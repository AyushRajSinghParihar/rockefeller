# Allocate a scarce operating resource

Use when choosing a product/customer mix or deciding whether to expand a binding resource. Confirm that the proposed resource actually limits fulfillment: a lightly used factory does not explain every shipment delay.

Establish the same decision period for available resource units, contribution, committed orders, and demand limits. Identify incremental cash costs, resource consumption, quality/service requirements, and any indivisible batches or setup costs. Avoid allocating fixed overhead to manufacture a marginal loss; include fixed costs that actually change with the choice.

## One binding resource

If one resource binds and units are otherwise interchangeable, compare incremental contribution per unit of that resource. Allocate to the strongest opportunity only up to its credible demand limit and existing commitments; then allocate remaining capacity. If demand can be served without exhausting the resource, the ratio alone is not a reason to reject positive incremental contribution.

Example: A earns $40 per unit and takes two labor hours; B earns $30 and takes one. B earns $30/hour versus A's $20/hour. With 100 hours, demand for at most 60 B and 40 A, and no other constraint, produce 60 B and 20 A. This uses 100 hours and produces $2,600 contribution. Producing 100 B would exceed demand; choosing A first would sacrifice contribution.

## Multiple constraints or indivisible choices

A single ratio may fail with simultaneous labor/machine constraints, minimum orders, setup changes, batch sizes, delivery deadlines, or linked products. Check the feasibility and contribution of plausible mixes. For small whole-unit cases, enumerate feasible alternatives with available computation; use an appropriate optimization method for a larger decision. State the objective and constraints so the result is reproducible. Do not claim an optimum from a single-resource ranking when another resource also binds.

Separately check liquidity and timing: the highest contribution mix may require unavailable working capital or violate a delivery commitment. If obligations make a financially attractive mix infeasible, explain the tradeoff and investigate an actual alternative rather than silently dropping the obligation.

## Price the next unit of capacity

Estimate what additional contribution the resource would enable at supported demand, including setup, learning, maintenance, working capital, and delivery timing. Once another resource or demand becomes limiting, extra capacity stops earning the same return. Compare overtime, rescheduling, outsourcing, a narrower service promise, and investment where relevant. Use the core capital-allocation guidance for affordability and material commitments.
